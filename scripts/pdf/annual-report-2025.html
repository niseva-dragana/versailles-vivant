Annual report 2025

Activities, figures and accounts for the year 1 January – 31 December 2025, approved by the general assembly on 14 March 2026.

A fictional association. Versailles Vivant was created for a university course project (Business Practice, FCSE/FINKI, Ss. Cyril and Methodius University, Skopje, 2025/2026). Its people, events and figures are invented; it is not affiliated with the Château de Versailles. The figures match those on the website's Impact page.

A word from the president

2025 was the year the association grew up a little. We passed six hundred members, ran twenty-four events, and kept three garden apprentices in training all year. It was also the year the box hedges reminded us what they cost: replacing the sections we lost to box blight and box tree moth was the largest unplanned expense we have had. Every figure below is the real figure, including the uncomfortable ones, because members fund almost all of it and have a right to see it.

Marie Laurent, President of the board

Three garden apprentices were funded throughout the year. Membership grew by 35 over the year, more slowly than in 2017–2019 but steadily.

Income and spending

Income and spending were both €145,000: the association ended the year balanced, with no surplus and no deficit. The two part-time coordinators are the only paid staff; their salaries are included in the lines they work on, mainly garden maintenance and public programmes.

What the box hedges cost

Of the €75,400 spent on garden maintenance, €28,600 went on the box hedges of the parterres we look after: €17,900 for the two full trims of the year (apprentice and contractor hours), €7,800 to replace sections lost to box blight and box tree moth caterpillars, and €2,900 for tools and green-waste removal.

Membership costs €9 a month (individual), €16 (family, up to five people at one address) or €40 (patron). Members can leave at any time.

Governance

The association is run by a board of seven unpaid members, elected each March by the general assembly. The board elects its president, treasurer and secretary. Two part-time staff run the day-to-day work.

Plans for 2026

  • A Garden Saturday every month, and about 4,000 bulbs planted in the parterre borders in October.
  • Keep three apprentices in training, and help each one towards permanent work in horticulture.
  • Free public talks in the 2026–2027 season: the fountains' hidden engineering, the Hall of Mirrors and the politics of 1919, and the King's building accounts.
  • Publish the archive volunteers' work on the inventories of 1793 as a members' talk.
